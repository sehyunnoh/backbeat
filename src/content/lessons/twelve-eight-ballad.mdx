You met triplets in Level 6: three notes per beat. A **12/8 feel** (twelve eighth notes per bar, grouped in threes) is built entirely from triplets. It gives slow songs a rolling, swaying feel and appears in many blues, soul and rock ballads.

You can think of it as **four beats, each split into three**. Count **1 trip-let 2 trip-let 3 trip-let 4 trip-let**.

## The groove

- **Hi-hat:** every triplet note, so **three per beat**. Louder on the number.
- **Snare:** beats 2 and 4.
- **Kick:** beats 1 and 3.

## How it differs from the shuffle

The shuffle (Level 6) skips the middle note of every triplet. The 12/8 groove plays **all three**. So the hi-hat is smooth and constant, while the shuffle bounces.

## Learning it

1. **Hi-hat only,** counting "1 trip-let 2 trip-let ...".
2. **Add the snare** on 2 and 4.
3. **Add the kick** on 1 and 3.
4. **Use alternating hands** on the hi-hat if you like: R L R, L R L, as in the triplets lesson. If it is too tangled, keep the right hand alone at 55 to 60 BPM.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Keep it slow and smooth">
This groove is not about speed. At 50 to 70 BPM, a steady, even hi-hat with a solid snare is exactly what makes a ballad feel good.
</Callout>

## Check yourself

- Do the three hi-hat notes in each beat sound equal?
- Does the snare land clearly on 2 and 4?
- Does the kick stay light and not rush?
