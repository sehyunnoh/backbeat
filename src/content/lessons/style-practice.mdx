You now know several grooves. The differences between them are often small: the snare or the kick moves, or the hi-hat changes. Seeing them side by side makes them much easier to remember.

## Compare the grooves

| Groove | Hi-hat or ride | Snare | Kick |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic rock (Level 3) | Eighth notes | 2 and 4 | 1 and 3 |
| Half-time (Level 5) | Eighth notes | 3 only | 1 (and extras) |
| Four on the floor (Level 5) | Open on the "&" | 2 and 4 | Every beat |
| Sixteenth hi-hat (Level 5) | Sixteenth notes | 2 and 4 | 1 and 3 |
| Shuffle (Level 6) | Triplets, skipping the middle | 2 and 4 | 1 and 3 |
| Funk-style (Level 8) | Sixteenth notes | 2 and 4, plus ghost notes | Syncopated |
| One drop (Level 8) | Eighth or quarter notes | 3 only | 3 only |
| 12/8 ballad (Level 8) | Every triplet | 2 and 4 | 1 and 3 |
| Swing (Level 8) | Ride: ding, ding-a | none (hi-hat foot on 2 and 4) | Feathered or none |

## One style per week

A simple way to keep learning:

1. **Pick one groove** for the week.
2. **Day 1:** play it slowly with the metronome.
3. **Day 2-3:** raise the tempo a little and add a fill at the end of every four bars.
4. **Day 4:** play along with a song in that style.
5. **Day 5:** record yourself and listen back.
6. **Weekend:** play it just for fun, without the metronome.

## Listen with new ears

When you listen to music, try to answer three questions about the drums:

1. Where is the **snare**: 2 and 4, only on 3, or somewhere else?
2. What does the **kick** do: steady, or moving around?
3. What is the **hi-hat or ride** playing: eighths, sixteenths, triplets?

<Callout kind="tip" title="A listening game">
Choose five songs and write down the groove for each: "snare on 2 and 4, kick on 1 and 3, eighth-note hi-hat". You will see the same few patterns over and over.
</Callout>
