The **shuffle** is the bouncy, skipping groove of blues, rock and roll and a lot of old soul music. It is built on the triplets from the last lesson, with one note left out.

## The idea

Count triplets: **1 trip-let 2 trip-let 3 trip-let 4 trip-let**. On the hi-hat, play the **first and third** note of each triplet and **skip the middle one**. That "long-short" pattern is the shuffle.

- **Hi-hat:** the first and third note of every triplet.
- **Snare:** beats 2 and 4.
- **Kick:** beats 1 and 3.

## Learn it in layers

1. **Counting only:** say "1 trip-let 2 trip-let" with the click until it is automatic.
2. **Hi-hat alone:** play the long-short pattern with your right hand: **1 (rest) let, 2 (rest) let**.
3. **Add the snare** on 2 and 4.
4. **Add the kick** on 1 and 3.

<Callout kind="note" title="Straight versus shuffled">
In the basic 8-beat the two notes in a beat are exactly equal. In the shuffle the second note comes later, at the end of the triplet. The notes sound uneven on purpose, and that is what makes it swing.
</Callout>

## Tips

- **Say the triplet words out loud.** "1 trip-let" with the "trip" silent is the whole groove.
- **Keep the hi-hat even.** The long-short pattern must be the same every beat.
- **Start slow,** at 70 BPM or lower. Shuffles feel relaxed, not rushed.
- **Listen for it** in blues songs. Once you know the sound, you will hear it everywhere.
