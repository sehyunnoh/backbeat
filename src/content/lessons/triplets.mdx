So far every beat was split into **two** (eighth notes) or **four** (sixteenth notes). A **triplet** splits one beat into **three** equal parts. It gives music a rolling, swinging feel.

## How to count them

Say **"1 trip-let, 2 trip-let, 3 trip-let, 4 trip-let"**. Each word gets one note, so you play three notes in every beat. The chart shows the count as **1 tr lt**.

| Note | Notes per beat | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Quarter | 1 | 1 2 3 4 |
| Eighth | 2 | 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & |
| Triplet | 3 | 1 trip-let 2 trip-let ... |
| Sixteenth | 4 | 1 e & a 2 e & a ... |

## Play them

Alternate hands the whole time: **R L R, L R L, R L R, L R L**. The hand that starts each beat changes, because there are three notes in a beat and your hands only have two sides. That is what gives triplets their lilt.

## Accent the beat

Play a louder note on each number. Because the starting hand alternates, the accent switches between the right and left hand: R, L, R, L.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Hearing the difference">
Tap your foot on the beat. Say "1 and 2 and" (two notes per beat) and then "1 trip-let 2 trip-let" (three notes per beat). Feel how the second one rolls. The foot stays steady while the subdivision changes.
</Callout>

## Common problems

- **Turning triplets into eighth notes:** the three notes must be evenly spaced. Say the words out loud.
- **Accents on the wrong hand:** the accent really does swap hands. That is normal, so do not fight it.
- **Rushing:** triplets at 50 BPM are not slow, so stay relaxed.
