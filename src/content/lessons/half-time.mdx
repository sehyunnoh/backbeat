Take the basic 8-beat and **move the snare from 2 and 4 to only beat 3**. The hi-hat keeps playing eighth notes at the same speed, but the beat now feels **half as fast**: heavy, slow and powerful. This is called **half-time**.

## The groove

<Pattern
  title="Half-time groove"
  description="Snare on 3 only. Kick on 1."
  bpm={70}
  minBpm={50}
  maxBpm={120}
  lessonId="half-time"
  hh="xxxxxxxx xxxxxxxx"
  sn="----o--- ----o---"
  bd="o------- o-------"
/>

Count 1 & 2 & **3** & 4 &. Only beat 3 has the snare. Beats 2 and 4 are now empty, apart from the hi-hat.

## Add a kick

Once the basic half-time groove is steady, try one extra kick on the "&" of 2:

<Pattern
  title="Half-time with an extra kick"
  description="Kick on 1 and on the & of 2."
  bpm={70}

  maxBpm={120}
  hh="xxxxxxxx xxxxxxxx"
  sn="----o--- ----o---"
  bd="o--o---- o--o----"
/>

## What to listen for

- The click feels like it is **twice as slow**, because the backbeat only comes every two beats.
- The hi-hat must not change at all. It is the heartbeat of the groove.
- A confident, loud snare on 3 gives the groove its weight.

<Callout kind="note" title="Half-time and half speed">
Half-time is not about playing slowly. The tempo is the same, but the snare placement changes how the beat feels. Songs use it for heavy choruses and ballads.
</Callout>

## Try it

Play the normal 8-beat for two bars, then switch to half-time for two bars, back and forth. Changing only the snare, without touching the hi-hat, trains your coordination.
