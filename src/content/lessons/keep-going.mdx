You started with the names of the drums. Now you can play grooves, fills, sixteenth notes, triplets, ghost notes and rudiments. That is a real foundation. Here are some directions for what next.

## Things to explore

| Direction | What it means | A good first step |
|---|---|---|
| **More rudiments** | The other standard rudiments such as five-stroke rolls, drags and flam combinations | Pick one a month and practise it a few minutes a day |
| **Reading drum music** | Learn standard drum notation | Look at a drum chart of a song you know while you listen |
| **Different styles** | Funk, jazz, Latin, metal, reggae and more | Pick a style you love and learn its basic groove |
| **Playing with others** | Jam with a friend or a band | Play along with a friend on guitar or keys |
| **Recording** | Record your playing | Use the module's C10 Recording, or a phone |
| **A teacher** | Someone to look at your technique and give feedback | A few lessons can fix habits early, when it is easy |

## Ideas for your own projects

- **Learn three songs** you love and play them from start to finish.
- **Write a fill** and use it in every song for a week.
- **Play along with the module's built-in songs,** choosing a new one each day.
- **Challenge yourself with a tempo goal:** 90 BPM on the sixteenth-note hi-hat beat, for example.

## When you feel stuck

- **Slow everything down.**
- **Go back one lesson.** The basics are never wasted.
- **Watch someone else** play the same thing. The video lists on each lesson are a good place to start.
- **Take a break** for a day or two, then come back.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Keep it fun">
You started because you were interested in drums. Keep some time every session just for playing what you enjoy.
</Callout>

## Thank you for practising here

This site is just a starting point. Keep your sticks nearby and play a little every day. See you on the kit.
