A solo that is full of notes all the time is tiring to listen to. The two tools that fix this are **space** (rests) and **dynamics** (loud and soft). With just these, a very simple pattern can sound dramatic.

## Space

A **rest** is silence. In a solo it is not an empty spot: it is a **pause for effect**, like a comma in a sentence. A single loud hit followed by silence can be more powerful than a hundred notes.

Remember to keep counting through every rest. The pulse is still going in your head.

## A solo that grows

This four-bar idea uses only the snare:

- **Bar 1:** one loud hit, then silence.
- **Bar 2:** very quiet notes (the small rings are ghost notes).
- **Bar 3:** medium notes, getting busier.
- **Bar 4:** loud, strong notes to finish.

## Tools for each bar

| Idea | How to do it |
|---|---|
| **Stop** | Play a loud hit and lift your sticks away. Hold the silence for the full count. |
| **Quiet** | Keep the stick low, a centimetre or two above the pad. |
| **Build** | Add a note or two each bar. |
| **Loud finish** | Lift the stick higher, and hit with confidence. |

<Callout kind="tip" title="Counting the silence">
When you play the stop, say "2, 3, 4, and" out loud in your head. If you look at the metronome light and the next sound comes in on the right beat, you are doing it right.
</Callout>
