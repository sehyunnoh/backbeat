Almost everyone has a stronger hand. The other one is a little slower, quieter or less accurate at first. That is normal, and it gets better quickly with a bit of attention.

Your goal is not to make the weak hand strong, it is to make the two **match**.

## Drill 1: lead with the other hand

You probably start every pattern with your right hand. Try starting with your left.

Then play the earlier right-lead version and compare. Do both feel equally smooth?

## Drill 2: one hand at a time

If one hand is clearly weaker, give it a little extra time:

1. Play four notes with your weak hand, then four with your strong one, over and over, at 60 BPM.
2. Listen: do the two groups sound the same?
3. Watch: do both sticks reach the same height?

## Use your module: Stroke Balance

Your TD-1 has a coach exercise for exactly this.

1. Press **Select** until the Coach lamp is lit.
2. Choose **C-8 Stroke Balance** with **[-]** and **[+]**.
3. Press **Metronome** to start.
4. Play single strokes on a pad. The display shows how hard each hit is, from hard to soft, so you can see if one hand is hitting harder.

Aim for the display to look the same on right and left hits.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Record and listen">
Coach menu **C10 Recording** lets you record yourself and play it back. Your ear often catches uneven hands better than your eyes do.
</Callout>

## Habits that help

- **Alternate the lead hand** in your warm-up every day.
- **Match the height.** When the weak hand is quieter, raise it, not the strong hand lower.
- **Do not just add speed.** Slow, even and relaxed beats fast and sloppy.
- **Be patient.** Weeks of small, regular practice make a clear difference.
