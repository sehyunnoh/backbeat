How loud a drum is depends mostly on **how high the stick starts**. A high start gives a loud note and a low start gives a soft one. The speed of the stick does the work, not your muscles.

Teachers name four basic strokes, based on where the stick starts and where it ends up:

<StrokeHeights />

Why this matters: when you play a loud note followed by a quiet one, the stick has to be in the right place for the next note. A **down stroke** drops it low for a quiet note. An **up stroke** lifts it for a loud one. You will use these constantly when you play grooves with accents.

## Practice 1: two heights

1. Play four soft notes with the stick about 3 cm (1 inch) above the pad. That is a **tap**.
2. Play four loud notes with the stick lifted about 15 cm (6 inches). That is a **full stroke**.
3. Use one hand at a time, wrist only.

Listen. The loud notes should be clearly louder. The soft ones should still sound clean, not dead.

## Practice 2: accent then soft

The chart plays one big note then three soft ones, with alternating hands. The accent comes from a high start, then the stick drops low for the quiet notes.

## Keep it simple

- Do not exaggerate heights yet. About 3 cm for quiet and 15 cm for loud is plenty.
- Keep the **same** height for notes that should match. Uneven heights are the most common reason beats sound sloppy.
- The wrist snap is the same movement for all four strokes. Only the start and end heights change.
