The easiest way to turn a rudiment into a solo is to **play it on different drums**. The sticking stays exactly the same: it is only where you hit that changes. The sound jumps in pitch and suddenly it sounds like music.

## Down the kit

Play eight alternating notes, two on each drum: **Snare, Tom 1, Tom 2, Tom 3**. The sticking is the same single stroke roll you know: R L R L R L R L.

## Down and back up

Play the same idea going down in bar 1, and back up in bar 2. The hands keep alternating all the way.

## How to practise

1. **Play it on one drum first.** Eight even strokes on the snare at 60 BPM.
2. **Move after every two notes.** Your eyes should look at the next drum before your stick arrives.
3. **Slow down for the moves.** Moving to a new drum is the hard part, so give it time.
4. **Let your hands cross if they need to.** At first it can feel awkward, and that is normal.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Look first, then play">
Your hand follows your eyes. Look at the next drum a beat before you hit it. This single habit makes the moves much smoother.
</Callout>

## Try other rudiments

Once the single strokes feel smooth, swap in another pattern and play the same route around the kit:

- **Paradiddle** (R L R R L R L L), moving every four notes.
- **Double strokes** (R R L L), moving every two notes.

Keep the tempo slower than you think you need. Clean and even sounds far better than fast and messy.
