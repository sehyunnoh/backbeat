Until now the hi-hat has been closed: short and tight. With your **left foot** on the hi-hat pedal, you can also open it, which gives a longer, sizzling sound.

## How the pedal works

- **Pedal pressed down:** the hi-hat is **closed**. Short, tight sound.
- **Pedal released:** the hi-hat is **open**. Longer, washier sound.
- Your TD-1DMK senses the pedal position, including half-open, so you get a smooth range between the two.

The TD-1 Owner's Manual also lists a **foot closed** sound (pressing the pedal on its own) and a **foot splash** (a quick press and release). You can try those on the module for fun.

## Step 1: feel the pedal

1. Rest your left foot on the pedal and press it down.
2. Tap the hi-hat with a stick. Short and tight.
3. Lift your heel slightly so the pedal rises. Tap again. Longer.
4. Go back and forth until you can hear the difference.

## Step 2: an open hi-hat in the beat

A very common trick: play the **last hi-hat note of the bar open** (on the "&" after 4) and close it again on 1 with your foot as the next bar begins.

Tips:

- **Lift** the pedal just before the "&" of 4, hit the hi-hat, then **press** the pedal down on 1.
- Closing the hi-hat on 1 also stops the open sound, so you hear a clean cut. That sounds good because the kick lands on 1 as well.
- Use **only your left foot** for the hi-hat pedal. It is the same foot, the same motion every bar.

## Step 3: open on every "&"

Once the one open note feels steady, try an open hi-hat on each "&". It sounds like a sizzling groove.

This one is harder, because your foot is now busy on every beat. Go slowly. At 55 BPM, count out loud and let the pedal move with your counting.

<Callout kind="note" title="It is a lot to do at once">
Your left foot is doing something new while both hands and the right foot are busy. If you lose everything, practise the foot movement alone with the hi-hat pedal, then add the hands.
</Callout>
