The **crash** cymbal is a big, bright splash. Drummers use it to say "something new starts here". It usually lands on **beat 1** of a new section, together with the kick.

## How it works

On beat 1, your right hand hits the **crash** instead of the hi-hat. Your right foot hits the kick at the same time. Then your right hand goes back to the hi-hat on the "&" and continues the beat.

## Step by step

1. **Practise the move alone:** play only the crash on 1 and the hi-hat on every "&" and number after that (no snare, no kick). Count out loud.
2. **Add the kick on 1:** crash and kick together. Do not worry if they are not perfectly together yet.
3. **Add the snare.**
4. **Loop:** play bar 1, bar 2, bar 1, bar 2, and listen for the crash coming back every two bars.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Hit the crash on the edge">
Hit the crash with the shoulder of the stick near the edge, a glancing blow, rather than flat on top. It rings better and you do not need to hit hard. The TD-1DMK crash senses both the bow and the edge, so the edge also sounds different.
</Callout>

## Common problems

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| The crash is late | Aim for it earlier. Count the "4 &" out loud and move your hand up on "&". |
| You forget to go back to the hi-hat | Practise the move alone first, then add the rest. |
| Reaching feels awkward | Move the crash a little closer or lower. Your posture lesson explains how to adjust distances. |
