Sheet music writes down two things: **which drum** and **when**. This lesson is about "when". The shape of a note tells you how long it lasts, and **rests** are shapes for silence.

You already use these every day, just under other names:

| Name | Beats in 4/4 | You count it as |
|---|---|---|
| Whole note | 4 | one long note (not on a drum, you just let it ring) |
| Half note | 2 | 1 (2) |
| Quarter note | 1 | 1 2 3 4 |
| Eighth note | one half | 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & |
| Sixteenth note | one quarter | 1 e & a 2 e & a ... |

On drums, you almost always see **quarter, eighth and sixteenth notes**.

## Quarter notes

One note per beat. A solid notehead with a stem. Here the snare plays on every beat.

## Eighth notes

Two notes per beat. Their stems are joined by a thick line called a **beam**.

## Sixteenth notes

Four notes per beat, joined by **two beams**.

## Rests

A **rest** means "be silent for this long". You keep counting through it. The shapes are different for each length.

<Notation
  title="Quarter rests"
  description="The kick plays on 1 and 3. The squiggles on 2 and 4 are quarter rests: silence for one beat."
  bd="o---o---"
/>

<Notation
  title="Eighth rests"
  description="The small slash with a dot on each number is an eighth rest. The notes only fall on the &."
  sn="-o-o-o-o"
/>

<Callout kind="tip" title="A rest is not a break">
Drummers sometimes treat rests as empty time. Treat them as part of the beat. Say "1" out loud on the rest, and play only on the "&".
</Callout>

## How to read the count

Under every staff on this site you will see the count: **1 & 2 &** or **1 e & a**. The bold numbers are the beats. Each note sits straight above its count. That is how you turn what you see into what you play.

## Try it

For each staff above: say the count, clap or tap the rhythm on one pad, and name the note type. Then check against the chart in the earlier lessons, where the same rhythms appear as grids.
