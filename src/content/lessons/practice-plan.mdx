Progress comes from **regular, focused** practice, not from long, aimless sessions. A simple plan helps you use your time and keeps you from always playing the same things.

## The shape of a session

A 25-minute session works well:

| Minutes | What | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Warm up | Shake out your hands, then one-hand quarter and eighth notes |
| 7 | Technique | A rudiment: single, double or paradiddle at a comfortable tempo |
| 8 | Groove | The beat you are working on, with the metronome |
| 5 | A song or fills | Play along, or practise a fill |
| 2 | Free play | Play what you like |

## A week

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Rudiments and the basic beat |
| Tuesday | Groove variations (kick, hi-hat, ride) |
| Wednesday | Fills |
| Thursday | Rudiments and dynamics |
| Friday | A song |
| Weekend | Rest, or play for fun |

You do not need to follow it exactly. What matters is that a session has a clear purpose.

## Use the module's timer

Your TD-1 has a **C-9 Timer** in Coach mode. It alerts you when time is up. The default is 3 minutes, and you can set 1 to 99 minutes by long-pressing **Metronome**. It is handy for keeping each part of a session to its time.

## Habits that help

- **Slow is fast.** Take any tricky part down to a tempo where it is easy.
- **Raise the tempo in small steps,** around 4 to 5 BPM, only when the lower tempo is comfortable.
- **Record yourself** every week or two, and listen back. It is the best way to hear progress.
- **Stop when you are tense.** A short break is better than practising badly.
- **Keep a log,** even one line a day: what you practised and the tempo.

<Callout kind="note" title="Be kind to yourself">
Some days it will feel like you got worse. That is normal, and it usually means you are about to improve. Keep showing up.
</Callout>
