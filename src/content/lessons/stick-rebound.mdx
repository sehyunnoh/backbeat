A drum is bouncy, and so is a stick. If you drop a stick on a drum head, it bounces back up by itself. **Good technique is staying out of the way of that bounce.**

Beginners usually do the opposite: they push the stick into the pad and hold it there. That feels powerful, but it kills the sound, tires your hand and makes fast playing impossible.

## Where to hit

- Aim for the **same spot** on the pad every time. Near the middle of the head is a good place.
- On the **snare**, stay off the rim for now. The snare pad senses the rim separately and plays a different sound, so rim hits will surprise you.
- The **toms** only sense the head, so no worry there.
- Hit with the **tip** of the stick, with the stick coming down at a slight angle, not straight down.

## The drop test

This is the best rebound exercise, and it needs no skill.

1. Hold the stick with your matched grip, loosely.
2. Let your hand rest on a pad, with the stick about 5 cm (2 inches) above the head.
3. Let the stick **drop** and bounce. Do not help it, do not hold it down.
4. Listen: you will hear a few small bounces, each quieter than the last.

That feeling is your goal: the stick does the bouncing and your hand just holds on lightly.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Hit and lift">
Think "hit and lift", never "hit and push". The stick should leave the pad as soon as it arrives.
</Callout>

## Try it

Do each step for 30 seconds, one hand at a time, at a relaxed pace without a click:

1. **Drop and catch:** let the stick drop and bounce, then lift it back up and drop again.
2. **Single taps:** hit once, let it rebound up by itself, and hit again when it reaches the top.
3. **Listen:** the notes should sound full and round. A flat, thuddy sound means you are pushing into the pad.

Next lesson adds the other half: the wrist that starts the motion.
