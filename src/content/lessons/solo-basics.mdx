When people picture a drum solo, they picture someone playing as fast as possible. A beginner solo is the opposite: **slow, clear and musical.** If you can play a short idea cleanly, repeat it and change it, you are already soloing.

You have done small solos already. Every fill you played is a one-beat solo.

## Five ground rules

1. **Keep the pulse.** Even when you play something different, you should feel the beat in your head. Use the metronome until it becomes automatic.
2. **Repeat before you change.** Play an idea, then play it again. A listener needs to hear something twice before it feels like it means something.
3. **Use space.** Rests are as important as notes. A good drum solo breathes.
4. **Build and release.** Start quietly and simply, build energy, then land somewhere clear.
5. **Keep it simple.** Four clean bars are better than sixteen messy ones.

## Keep the pulse with your foot

A very helpful way to practise this is to let your **right foot play every beat** on the kick, while your hands play an idea. The foot is your steady heartbeat. The hands are free to tell a story on top.

Play the kick first and count out loud. When the foot feels automatic, add the hands.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Four limbs, one pulse">
If you lose the kick whenever your hands do something new, slow down. Play the kick plus **only the snare notes**, then add the tom notes.
</Callout>

## What you will learn in this level

| Lesson | The skill |
|---|---|
| Around the drums | Move simple sticking patterns across the snare and toms |
| Call and response | Ask a question with the drums, then answer it |
| Space and dynamics | Use rests and different volumes |
| Triplet flow | Move triplets around the toms |
| Build a solo | Put the ideas together into four bars |

## Try it

Play the exercise above for two minutes. Then change one note in the hand pattern, and play it again. You just made a variation.
