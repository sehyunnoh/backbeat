The **double stroke roll** has two hits per hand before you switch: **R R L L R R L L**. It sounds smooth and "buzzy" at speed, and it is the sticking behind many fills.

## The idea

At slow tempos you play each of the four notes as its own small wrist stroke: R, R, L, L. As you speed up, your hand cannot lift for every note, so you start using the **rebound** from the stick: the first stroke comes from the wrist, and the stick bounces back for the second.

You are not forcing two hits. You are letting the stick bounce once, with a little help from your fingers.

## How to practise

1. **Play slowly first.** At 50 BPM treat each hit as a separate wrist stroke. Aim for four identical notes.
2. **Make the second stroke match the first.** The second hit of a pair is often weaker. Listen for that.
3. **Raise the tempo in small steps.** Go up 5 BPM when it feels easy. When you can no longer lift for each note, let the stick bounce for the second one.
4. **Single-hand first.** If it is hard, play only the right hand pairs: R R, rest, R R, rest. Then the left hand.

## Check yourself

- Are the two hits of each pair the same volume?
- Is the gap between pairs the same as the gap inside pairs?
- Are you tense? Tension makes the bounce stop. If you feel it, slow down.
