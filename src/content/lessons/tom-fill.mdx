Your kit has **three toms**. Toms sound lower as you go from Tom 1 to Tom 3, so moving across them sounds like a falling melody. That is the classic tom fill sound.

## The fill

The fill uses beats 3 and 4 of the bar: **Tom 1, Tom 1, Tom 2, Tom 3**, all eighth notes. The groove plays for beats 1 and 2 (hi-hat, snare on 2, kick on 1), then your hands leave the hi-hat for the toms.

Look at the chart: each tom gets its own row, so you can see the fill move down the page.

## Learn it in pieces

1. **Fill alone, on the pads:** Tom 1, Tom 1, Tom 2, Tom 3, counting "3 & 4 &". Start by alternating hands (R L R L). If a move feels awkward or your hands cross, use whichever hand is closest to that tom. There is no single correct sticking.
2. **Slow motion first:** at 50 BPM, look at each tom as you go. Your eyes help your hands.
3. **Add the groove before it:** beats 1 and 2, then straight into the fill.
4. **Add the crash after:** land on the crash and kick together.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Mind the rim">
The toms only have a head sensor, so a rim hit just plays the head sound or may not register. Hit the middle of the pad, not the edge.
</Callout>

## Make your own

Once you are comfortable, change the notes: Tom 1, Tom 2, Tom 3, Tom 3. Or start on the snare. Keep four notes and keep them even. There are lots of fills made from just those few drums.
