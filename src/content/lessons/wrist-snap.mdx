The wrist snap is the main engine of drumming. Your forearm stays calm, and your wrist works like a door hinge: **down to hit, up to rebound**.

<WristSnapAnim />

## How it feels

1. **Start with the stick up**, tip a little above your hand, wrist slightly lifted.
2. **Snap the wrist down**, like you are flicking water off your fingers. The stick hits the pad.
3. **Let it bounce.** The stick springs back up. Your wrist follows it up.
4. Repeat. It becomes a small, relaxed up-and-down swing.

Your arm should barely move. If your elbow and shoulder are doing the work, make the movement smaller and use more wrist.

<Callout kind="note" title="Use the whole hand later">
Once you play faster, fingers help the rebound too. For now, wrist only, so you learn the hinge.
</Callout>

## What to check

- **Sound:** every hit should sound the same, full and round.
- **Look:** the stick goes up about the same height each time.
- **Feel:** no tight forearm. If it burns, rest and loosen your grip.
- **Timing:** each hit lands on the click, not just before or just after.

## Tempo ladder

Stay at each tempo until you can play two bars in a row cleanly:

| Step | BPM | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50 | Smooth and relaxed |
| 2 | 60 | Same relaxed feeling |
| 3 | 70 | Keep the wrist loose |
| 4 | 80 | Only if everything still feels easy |

There is no prize for speed. Smooth now means fast later.
