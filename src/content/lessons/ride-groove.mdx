The **ride** cymbal rings out and sustains, so it sounds bigger and more open than the hi-hat. Drummers often switch from the hi-hat to the ride when a song gets bigger, like a chorus.

It is the same beat. Your right hand simply moves from the hi-hat to the ride.

## Ride on quarter notes

A calmer version plays the ride only on the beats. It leaves more space, which is great for slower songs.

## Playing the ride

- **Hit the bow.** On your TD-1DMK, play the flat top of the cymbal pad near the middle-to-outer area. The ride senses the bow and the edge, so the edge sounds different. For a steady ride, stay on the bow.
- **Let it ring.** Cymbals sound best when you hit lightly and let the stick bounce off.
- **Keep the stick relaxed.** Same wrist snap as always.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Hi-hat or ride?">
Try both on the same beat and listen. The hi-hat is tight and busy, the ride is open and washy. There is no right choice, you pick what suits the song.
</Callout>

## Practice

1. Play the 8-beat on the hi-hat for one minute.
2. Switch your right hand to the ride at the start of a bar, without stopping.
3. Switch back. Practise the move until it does not break the beat.
