**Funk** is built on a tight, busy groove where the drums lock together. Many funk grooves share the same ingredients, and you already know all of them:

- a **sixteenth-note hi-hat** (Level 5),
- a **snare backbeat** on 2 and 4 with **ghost notes** around it (Level 6),
- a **kick that moves around** instead of always sitting on 1 and 3 (Level 3).

This is not any one famous groove. It is a simple example that uses those ingredients.

## The groove

Count **1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a**.

- **Hi-hat:** sixteenth notes, a little louder on the numbers.
- **Snare:** loud on 2 and 4, with a very quiet ghost note on the "a" of 2 and the "a" of 4.
- **Kick:** on 1, on the "&" of 2 and on the "&" of 3.

## Learn it in layers

1. **Hi-hat alone** at 50 BPM until it is relaxed.
2. **Add the snare backbeat** (just 2 and 4).
3. **Add the kick,** which makes the groove syncopated, so it feels a little off the beat.
4. **Add the ghost notes** last. They are small taps with a low stick.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Do it one limb at a time">
If the kick is hard, mute the hi-hat and snare with the row names and play only the kick while you count "1 e & a". Then add the others back one by one.
</Callout>

## What makes it funky

- **Space and weight:** a heavy backbeat and light ghost notes make the groove sound like it is breathing.
- **Syncopation:** kick notes that land between the beats give a push and pull.
- **Locking in:** every note is exactly in time, so playing with the metronome is the whole game.

## Make it yours

Move one kick note somewhere else on the sixteenth-note grid and listen. Keep the backbeat steady, and decide if you like the result.
