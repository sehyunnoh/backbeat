This is **the** drum beat. It is behind thousands of pop and rock songs and it is the best thing to practise for months. Once you can play it comfortably, you can play along with a lot of music.

The only change from the last lesson: the hi-hat plays **twice as often**, on every number and every "&".

## The beat

Count **1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**.

- **Hi-hat:** every number and every "&".
- **Snare:** on 2 and 4.
- **Kick:** on 1 and 3.

## Make the hi-hat sound better

A real drummer does not play every hi-hat note at the same volume. Playing the "numbers" a little louder than the "&" gives the beat a bounce:

<Pattern
  title="8-beat with hi-hat accents"
  description="The big × is a slightly louder hi-hat. It lands on every number."
  bpm={70}
  minBpm={50}
  maxBpm={120}
  hh="XxXxXxXx XxXxXxXx"
  sn="--o---o- --o---o-"
  bd="o---o--- o---o---"
/>

Use stick height (from the stroke types lesson) to do it: slightly higher on the numbers, lower on the "&".

## Tempo ladder

Move up only when the lower step is comfortable and relaxed.

| Step | BPM | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 | Play for one minute without stopping |
| 2 | 70 | Same |
| 3 | 80 | Keep the hi-hat even |
| 4 | 90 | Stay relaxed, check shoulders and hands |

<Callout kind="note" title="Common problems">
**Hi-hat uneven:** your right hand tires. Loosen your grip and use the wrist.
**Rushing:** your hands race ahead of the click. Slow down and count out loud.
**Snare too soft:** snare on 2 and 4 is the backbeat, so give it a clear, confident hit.
</Callout>

## Check yourself

Record yourself with the module's **C10 Recording** Coach mode (or your phone) and listen back. Does the hi-hat stay steady? Do the snare hits land together with the click on 2 and 4?
