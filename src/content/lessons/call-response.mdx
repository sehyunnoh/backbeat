Good solos sound like a conversation. A **call** is a short phrase. The **response** is an answer to it. The simplest answer is the **same rhythm on a different drum**. The listener recognises the rhythm, and the new sound makes it feel like a reply.

## One idea, four bars

Here is a one-bar rhythm. It plays on the snare as the call, on Tom 1 as the answer, then repeats, and finally the answer goes lower to Tom 3 to finish.

## Make it yours

1. **Invent a call.** Pick four or five notes on the eighth-note grid, such as "1, 2, 3 and 4". Say it out loud, then play it on the snare.
2. **Answer it.** Play the same rhythm on a tom.
3. **Change the ending.** Keep the first notes the same, but change the last one. This is the same idea with a small twist.
4. **Keep the pulse.** The kick on every beat, as in the first lesson, keeps everything together.

<Callout kind="note" title="Why it works">
People like patterns that repeat, and they like small surprises. A repeated rhythm with a new sound or a changed ending gives them both.
</Callout>

## Common problems

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| You lose count in the quiet bar | The answer bar has rests in it. Keep counting 1 & 2 & out loud. |
| The call and the answer sound different | Check that the rhythm is the same, note for note. Only the drum should change. |
| It feels stiff | Play it more lightly and let the sticks bounce. |
