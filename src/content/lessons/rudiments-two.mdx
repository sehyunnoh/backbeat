You already have the single stroke roll, the double stroke roll and the paradiddle. Here are two more that are worth adding.

## Paradiddle-diddle

The **paradiddle-diddle** is a paradiddle with a second pair at the end: **R L R R L L**. It has six notes, and the right hand leads every time, so it fits into groups of **three beats**: three beats of eighth notes.

Because it has six notes, the chart below uses **3 beats per bar**. Count **1 & 2 & 3 &**, and put the accent on the first note of each group.

Tips:

- **Say it out loud:** "Par-a-did-dle-did-dle".
- **Notice the two doubles** in the middle and end (R R, L L). Let them bounce.
- **Set the metronome to a 3-beat bar** (on the Metronome page, choose 3 beats per bar).

## The flam

A **flam** is two notes played almost together: a very quiet **grace note** a hair before a normal, louder **main note**. Together they make a fat, wide sound, like one note with a little push on the front of it.

- **Grace note:** the stick starts very low, close to the pad, and plays quietly.
- **Main note:** the other stick starts high and plays at normal volume.
- The two sticks hit almost together: the grace note just before the main note.

Practise a **right flam** (left grace note, right main note) and a **left flam** (right grace note, left main note), slowly, with the metronome on the beat.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Start with one hand low, one hand high">
Hold one stick low (about 2 cm above the pad) and the other high (about 15 cm). Drop both together. The low stick lands first and the high stick lands slightly after, and that is a flam.
</Callout>

## A short list of rudiments to explore next

There are about 40 standard rudiments. After these, the next ones to look up are:

- **Five-stroke roll:** two doubles and a single stroke.
- **Drag** (also called a ruff): two quiet grace notes before a main note.
- **Nine-stroke roll:** a longer roll made from doubles.

You do not need to learn them all. Pick one a month and play it for a few minutes every day.
