The **swing ride pattern** is the heartbeat of jazz drumming. It is played on the ride cymbal with a bouncy triplet feel, and it is often described with the words **"ding, ding-a, ding, ding-a"**.

It uses the triplet count you already know: **1 trip-let 2 trip-let 3 trip-let 4 trip-let**.

## The pattern

On the ride cymbal:

- **Beat 1:** one note.
- **Beat 2:** a note on the beat, plus a note on the last triplet of the beat ("let").
- **Beat 3:** one note.
- **Beat 4:** a note on the beat, plus a note on the last triplet ("let").

At the same time, your **left foot plays the hi-hat pedal on beats 2 and 4**, a soft "chick" sound.

## Learn it in steps

1. **Ride alone:** say "ding, ding-a, ding, ding-a" and play it on the ride with the metronome at 80 BPM.
2. **Foot alone:** play the hi-hat pedal on 2 and 4 with your left foot, counting out loud.
3. **Put them together** slowly. This is the hard part: your hand and foot must stay independent.
4. **Raise the tempo** slowly.

<Callout kind="note" title="Light and relaxed">
Swing should feel light, not hard. Hit the ride gently on the bow and let it ring. The pedal "chick" should also be soft. It is more about feel than volume.
</Callout>

## Make it jazzier

- **The second note** in "ding-a" is slightly lighter than the one on the beat.
- Add a **very soft kick** on every beat, called feathering. Keep it so quiet it is felt more than heard.
- Add **snare notes** at random places once the ride is automatic. Jazz drummers comment on the band this way. That is for another day.

## Common problems

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| It sounds like straight eighth notes | The "a" note comes late, at the third triplet. Say "trip-let" out loud. |
| The foot and hand drift apart | Practise the foot alone for a minute first. |
| The ride is too loud | Lower your stick height and let the cymbal ring. |
