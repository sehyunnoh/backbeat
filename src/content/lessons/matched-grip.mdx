**Matched grip** means both hands hold the stick the same way. It is the standard grip and the one this course uses. Your left hand does exactly what your right hand does.

The whole idea is a stick that can **swing and bounce**. Everything about grip comes from that.

## Check your grip

Hold a stick in one hand and look at it:

- **Is the stick pinched at one spot** with thumb and index finger, and loose everywhere else?
- **Can the tip swing** up and down freely if you tilt your hand?
- **Are your fingers curled loosely**, not clenched? You should be able to wiggle the end of the stick between your fingers.
- **Is your wrist neutral**, with the back of your hand roughly flat, not bent sharply up or down?

## Common mistakes

| What you do | What goes wrong | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Squeeze the whole stick | The stick cannot bounce. Your hand tires and may hurt. | Hold only at the fulcrum. Loosen the rest. |
| Hold too far back | The stick feels heavy and clumsy. | Slide your hand toward the balance point. |
| Fingers sticking out straight | You lose control of the stick. | Curl the last three fingers gently around it. |
| Death-grip because you are nervous | Tension travels up your arm. | Breathe out, let your shoulders drop, then re-grip. |

## Try it: the shake test

1. Hold a stick in your right hand using the grip above.
2. Let your forearm rest on your leg, tip of the stick pointing up.
3. Tilt your wrist gently so the tip nods down and up. It should move freely, like a seesaw on the fulcrum.
4. If it feels stuck, you are squeezing. Loosen your fingers and try again.
5. Repeat with the left hand. Your weaker hand will probably feel clumsier. That is normal.

Do this for about a minute each hand.
