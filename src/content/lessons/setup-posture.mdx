How you sit changes how you play. A good seat position means less strain and cleaner technique, so it is worth five minutes before your first real exercise.

<PostureDiagram />

## Set up in this order

1. **Throne height.** Sit down and let your feet rest on the kick pedal and hi-hat pedal. Your thighs should be about level, or sloping slightly down. If your knees are higher than your hips, raise the throne.
2. **Distance.** Sit so your elbows are loosely bent when the sticks rest on the snare. You should not be reaching or leaning forward to hit it.
3. **Snare height.** The snare head should be low enough that your forearms are about level with the floor when the sticks touch it. Many people set it near the top of their thighs or a little above.
4. **Toms and cymbals.** Move them close enough to reach without twisting. If you need to lean to hit something, move it closer.
5. **Feet.** Left foot on the hi-hat pedal, right foot on the kick pedal. Rest the foot on the footboard.

## Two ways to use the kick pedal

Both are normal and your manual lists them:

- **Heel up:** the whole leg works. Stronger sound, common in rock and pop.
- **Heel down:** the heel stays on the board. Finer control of volume.

Try both and see which feels natural. For now, either is fine.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Listen to your body">
Practise in short sessions, 10 to 20 minutes, and take breaks. If your hands, wrists or back start to hurt, or you feel tight, stop. Aches usually mean something about your set up or grip needs changing, not that you should push through. If it keeps happening, ask a drum teacher or a doctor.
</Callout>

## Also protect your ears

With an electronic kit, the loudest thing is often your headphones. Keep the volume where you can hear the click and the pads without it feeling loud.

## Try it

Sit down, set up in the order above, then play slow single taps on the snare for 30 seconds. Check three things as you play: shoulders down, wrists loose, breathing normally.
