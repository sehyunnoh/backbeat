Earlier fills used eighth notes, two notes per beat. A **sixteenth-note fill** packs **four notes into one beat**, so it sounds quick and exciting. The easiest place to try it is the **last beat of the bar** (beat 4).

## Snare fill on beat 4

Bar 1 starts with a crash and plays the groove. Bar 2 plays the groove for three beats and then fills beat 4 with four snare notes: **4 e & a**, alternating hands (R L R L).

## Tom fill on beat 4

The same fill, but the four notes travel around the toms: **Tom 1, Tom 1, Tom 2, Tom 3**.

## How to learn it

1. **Fill alone:** play the four notes on the snare at 50 BPM, counting "4 e & a" out loud.
2. **Add beat 3:** play the groove up to beat 3, then the fill.
3. **Add the crash:** land on the crash and kick on the next 1.
4. **Switch to toms:** same rhythm, different drums.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Speed comes later">
The four fill notes are twice as fast as the eighth-note fills. Playing them slowly and evenly sounds better than a fast, uneven fill.
</Callout>

## Common problems

- **The fill gets rushed:** it should fill exactly one beat, and the next crash lands on the click.
- **Uneven notes:** use the same stick height for all four notes.
- **Losing the beat:** keep counting "1 e & a" for the whole fill.
