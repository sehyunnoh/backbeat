A drum kit looks like a lot of things to hit. It is actually just a few sounds, and most beats use only three of them: **kick**, **snare** and **hi-hat**. Everything else is for colour and fills.

Here is your TD-1DMK as you would see it from above while sitting down.

<KitDiagram />

## What each pad is made of

Your kit is a "Double Mesh Kit": the snare and toms have woven mesh heads that feel like real drums and are much quieter than rubber pads.

| Part | Size and type | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Snare | 8" mesh | Senses both the head and the rim. A hit on the rim makes a different sound. |
| Toms 1, 2, 3 | 6" mesh | Head only. There is no rim sound on the toms. |
| Crash, Ride | 10" cymbal pads | Sense the bow (the flat top) and the edge. |
| Hi-hat | 10" cymbal pad + pedal | One fixed pad. The pedal tells the module if it is closed, half-open or open. |
| Kick | Kick pad | You hit it with a kick pedal. |

<Callout kind="note" title="Not in the box">
The TD-1DMK does not come with a kick pedal or a drum throne (stool). You need both to play. If you do not have them yet, they are the first things to get.
</Callout>

## Which hand or foot?

- **Hands** (with sticks): snare, toms, crash, ride, and the hi-hat pad.
- **Right foot**: kick pedal.
- **Left foot**: hi-hat pedal.

That is four limbs doing four different jobs. Do not worry about that yet. For now we just learn the names.

## Try it

1. Sit at the kit with your sticks.
2. Point at a part and say its name out loud. Then give it one gentle tap.
3. Go in the order of the numbers on the map: Crash, Tom 1, Tom 2, Ride, Hi-hat, Snare, Tom 3.
4. Press the kick pedal and the hi-hat pedal once each with your feet.

Do it twice. It will feel slow and silly, and that is exactly right.
