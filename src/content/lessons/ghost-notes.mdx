A **ghost note** is a very quiet note, usually on the snare. You almost feel it more than you hear it. Placed just before the loud snare backbeat, it adds a skip and a swagger that a plain beat does not have.

## How to play a ghost note

- **Hold the stick very low,** only a centimetre or two above the pad (this is the tap stroke from the stroke types lesson).
- **Let it rebound,** with a tiny wrist movement.
- **The backbeat** that follows is a full stroke, played from high. The stick goes from low to high between the two notes, the same motion as an up stroke.

The gap in volume between the ghost note and the backbeat is what you are aiming for.

## The groove

This uses a sixteenth-note grid, so count **1 e & a 2 e & a**. The ghost note sits on the **"a" of 1**, right before the snare on 2, and again on the "a" of 3, right before the snare on 4.

In the chart, the small ring on the snare row is a ghost note.

## Learn it in steps

1. **Snare only:** play ghost, backbeat, ghost, backbeat at 50 BPM. Make the two volumes very different.
2. **Add the hi-hat** on every eighth note.
3. **Add the kick** on 1 and 3.
4. **Raise the tempo** slowly.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Check your volume">
Your module's Coach menu **C-8 Stroke Balance** shows how hard each hit is, so it is useful for checking that your ghost notes are really quiet. You can also record yourself with **C10 Recording** and listen back.
</Callout>

## Common problems

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| The ghost notes are too loud | Lower the stick even more, and use less wrist. |
| The ghost notes disappear | A ghost note is quiet, but it still has to be a clear tap. |
| The backbeat is weak | Raise the stick for the backbeat, and hit with confidence. |
| The timing is sloppy | Slow down, and count 1 e & a out loud. |
