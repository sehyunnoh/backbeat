You do not need to read sheet music to start. This site uses a simple chart that you can follow with your eyes and ears.

## Count 1 2 3 4

Start the metronome at 70 BPM and count out loud with it: **"1, 2, 3, 4, 1, 2, 3, 4"**. One click, one number. The first beat of every bar is louder.

<Metronome bpm={70} />

Keep going for a minute. Counting out loud feels strange, but it is the thing that keeps your hands honest.

## Splitting the beat: "1 & 2 &"

Now put a second note between each click. The extra notes are the "and" (&):

**"1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &"**

You are now counting eighth notes: two per beat. Press play and watch the chart. The column that lights up is the note you are hearing. The hi-hat plays on every number and every "&".

## Reading the chart

- Each **row** is one drum. Tap a row name to mute it.
- Each **column** is a moment in time. The numbers on top are the count.
- A symbol means "hit here". A tiny dot means "rest".
- Cymbals and hi-hat use **×**. Drums use **●**. A big symbol is an accent.
- When there are two bars, they are stacked, read top to bottom.

## Your first groove (just listen)

This is the most common drum pattern in pop and rock. You will learn to play it in Level 3, so for now just press play, count out loud, and notice where each drum lands:

- **Hi-hat** on every eighth note.
- **Snare** on beats 2 and 4. This is the backbeat.
- **Kick** on beats 1 and 3.

## Try it

1. Count 1 2 3 4 with the metronome for one minute.
2. Count 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & at 70 BPM for one minute.
3. Play the groove above and count out loud along with it. You are not playing it, you are learning to hear it.
