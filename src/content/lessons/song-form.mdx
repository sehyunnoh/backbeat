Almost every song you know is built from the same few blocks. If you can hear them, a whole song becomes a handful of small pieces instead of one long wall of notes.

## The common blocks

| Block | What it is | What drummers often do |
|---|---|---|
| **Intro** | The opening, often short | Start quietly, or with a fill |
| **Verse** | The part where the story is told | Simpler groove, lighter energy |
| **Chorus** | The big, catchy part, repeated | Bigger sound, louder, maybe ride or open hi-hat |
| **Bridge** | A change of scene, usually once | Something different, such as half-time |
| **Outro** | The ending | Fade out or finish with a crash |

## Phrases come in fours and eights

Each block is usually **4, 8 or 16 bars** long. You practised exactly this in the groove, fill, groove lesson: three bars of groove, then a fill in bar four.

- A **fill** often lands at the end of a block, just before the change.
- A **crash** often lands on beat 1 of the next block.

That is why fills and crashes sit at the same spots in so many songs.

## Listening exercise

Put on a song you like, and listen three times:

1. **First time:** tap your foot and find the beat.
2. **Second time:** count **1 2 3 4** and notice where the count feels like it starts over every four bars.
3. **Third time:** write down when the song changes, with the bar numbers: "Intro 4 bars, verse 8 bars, chorus 8 bars..."

## What this gives you

With this map, learning a song is easy to plan: practise the groove for the verse, a different groove for the chorus, and the fills that connect them. The next lesson shows how.
