Playing with music is the most fun way to practise, and it teaches you to listen. Your TD-1 gives you two ways to do it.

## Option 1: the 15 built-in songs

1. Press **Select** until the **Song** lamp is lit.
2. Choose a song with **[-]** and **[+]**.
3. Press **Metronome** to play and to stop. You get a one-measure count-in.

The song tempo cannot be changed, so pick one that feels comfortable. Start with the slowest one you can find.

<Callout kind="note" title="Click on or off?">
If the metronome click gets in the way while a song plays, the manual explains how to switch it off: long-press **Metronome** and change the setting with **[-]** and **[+]**.
</Callout>

## Option 2: your own music

The **MIX IN** jack on the module takes a phone or music player. Plug it in with a cable and you hear your music and your drums together in the headphones. The module does not record the MIX IN sound, and it does not control the player, so start the music on your phone.

## What to play

Do not try to copy the real drummer. Start by playing the **basic 8-beat** all the way through.

1. **Find beat 1.** Listen to the song and tap your foot until you can feel the pulse.
2. **Count 1 2 3 4** with the music for one verse.
3. **Join in with just the hi-hat,** then add snare on 2 and 4, then add the kick.
4. **Add a fill** at the end of a phrase, if you like. Songs often change after four or eight bars.

## Choosing songs

- Pick songs with a **steady, medium tempo** and a clear backbeat.
- Songs with a simple drum part work best. Slow rock and pop songs are usually a good fit.
- If a song is too fast, ask your phone player for a slower one, or just play half the notes (for example, the hi-hat on every beat instead of every eighth).

## Another practice partner

Coach menu **C-3 Change-up** changes the rhythm every two measures, which makes you stay alert. It is a good way to practise changing your pattern without losing the beat.

## You made it

You started with the names of the drums and ended with a groove, a fill and a song. Keep practising a little every day and keep going. There is a lot more to learn: other rhythms, more rudiments, playing your favourite songs. You now have the foundation to do it.
