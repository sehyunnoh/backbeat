The **paradiddle** is a four-note pattern: **R L R R**, and then it flips to **L R L L**. It mixes single and double strokes, and it is one of the most useful patterns in drumming because the lead hand switches every time.

Say it out loud as you play. The name even fits the rhythm: **"Par-a-did-dle"** = R L R R.

## The pattern

Eight notes make one full paradiddle, R L R R L R L L. The accent (louder note) falls on the first note of each group of four.

## How to practise

1. **Say the sticking out loud.** "Right left right right, left right left left."
2. **Play slow, 50 to 60 BPM.** Most mistakes come from rushing the pair of same-hand notes.
3. **Make the accent clear.** The first note of each group is louder, the others quiet. That means a high stick on the accent, low sticks on the rest. This is the stroke types lesson in action.
4. **Break it into two halves.** If the full pattern is hard, practise R L R R four times, then L R L L four times, then join them.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Why it is useful">
Because the paradiddle swaps its lead hand, you will feel your weaker hand taking the accent just as often as your strong hand. That is the reason it is good practice, and why it shows up in so many fills.
</Callout>

## Common problems

| Problem | Likely reason | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Notes 3 and 4 (R R) are uneven | The second hit is weaker | Let the stick bounce for the second R, and keep the height low |
| Stumbling at the switch | Hands think they should alternate | Play only the sticking, slowly, and say it aloud |
| Everything the same volume | No height difference | Exaggerate the accent a bit, then bring it back |

## Check yourself

Sit up and relax. Play the pattern for one minute at 60 BPM. Count how many times you lose it. Next day try to beat that number.
