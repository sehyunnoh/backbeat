A **fill** is a short break from the groove, usually at the end of a phrase. It announces "the next section is coming", and it lands on the **crash** on beat 1.

The simplest fill is four eighth notes on the snare during **beats 3 and 4**.

## The fill

In bar 2, the hi-hat stops after beat 2 and the snare plays **3, 3&, 4, 4&**. Hit those four with alternating hands (R L R L). The kick plays on 1 only. Then bar 1 starts again with the crash.

## Learn it in pieces

1. **Fill alone:** play only the four snare notes (R L R L) at 60 BPM, counting "3 & 4 &" out loud.
2. **Beats 1 and 2 plus fill:** play the groove for beats 1 and 2 (hi-hat, snare on 2, kick on 1), then go straight into the fill.
3. **Add the crash:** after the fill, hit crash + kick on the next 1.
4. **Loop it:** groove, fill, crash, groove, fill, crash.

<Callout kind="note" title="Where does the hi-hat go?">
When your hands move to the snare for the fill, the hi-hat simply stops. That is normal. Your left foot can rest, or keep the hi-hat pedal closed.
</Callout>

## Typical stumbles

- **Rushing the fill:** the fill is the same speed as the groove. Count "3 & 4 &" carefully.
- **Stopping after the fill:** keep going into the crash on 1. Hold the beat through the transition.
- **Going loud and sloppy:** a clean, even fill sounds better than a fast, wild one.
