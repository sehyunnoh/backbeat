Playing hands and feet together feels impossible the first time, because your brain wants to do the same thing with every limb. The fix is to **build the beat in layers**. Add one limb at a time and only move on when the last layer feels easy.

## The plan

- **Right hand:** hi-hat.
- **Left hand:** snare.
- **Right foot:** kick pedal.

(If you are left-handed, you can mirror it. Many left-handed players still start with the hi-hat in the right hand, so try both and see what feels natural.)

The pattern is the one you heard in the counting lesson, at its simplest: hi-hat on every beat, snare on **2 and 4**, kick on **1 and 3**.

## Layer 1: hi-hat

Play the hi-hat with your right hand on every number. Count out loud: **1, 2, 3, 4**.

## Layer 2: add the snare

Keep the hi-hat going. Now hit the snare with your left hand on **2 and 4**. The hi-hat and snare land at the same time on those beats.

## Layer 3: add the kick

Now press the kick pedal with your right foot on **1 and 3**. All three drums are playing now.

Tap the **Kick** or **Snare** row name to mute it and play just the parts you are not playing yet.

## If it falls apart

| What happens | Try this |
|---|---|
| Your hi-hat speeds up when the snare comes in | Count out loud and let the hi-hat be the steady one. |
| You lose the kick | Practise kick + snare only (mute the hi-hat), then add the hi-hat back. |
| Both hands hit at the wrong time | Slow down to 50 BPM. Nobody ever got worse from going slower. |
| Everything feels stiff | Breathe out and check your shoulders. |

<Callout kind="tip" title="Count out loud">
Counting is not just for beginners. It is how you keep your hands and feet from drifting. Say "1, 2, 3, 4" as you play, even if it feels silly.
</Callout>

## Try it

Spend three minutes on each layer. When layer 3 feels easy for a whole minute at 60 BPM, go to the next lesson, where the hi-hat gets busier.
