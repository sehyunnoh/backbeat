A **rudiment** is a basic sticking pattern, a letter of the drummer's alphabet. The **single stroke roll** is the first one: **R L R L R L R L**, one hit per hand, always alternating.

It sounds simple, and it is, but making both hands sound identical is the hard part.

## The pattern

Count eighth notes: **1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**. Play one stroke on each. Right hand on the numbers, left hand on the "&".

## How to practise

1. **Start slow.** 60 BPM or below. Slow is where you build habits.
2. **Same height, both hands.** Watch the sticks. Lift them equally.
3. **Listen for evenness.** If you hear "LONG short LONG short", one hand is hitting harder. Quiet down the louder hand.
4. **Let it rebound.** Each hand uses the wrist snap from the last lesson. You are two independent bouncing sticks.
5. **Keep counting out loud.**

<Callout kind="tip" title="Try it with accents">
Play a louder note on the first of every group of four and keep the other three soft: **R** L R L, **R** L R L. It trains you to control stick height in both hands.
</Callout>

## Going faster, later

When eighth notes at 70 to 80 BPM feel easy and even, you can try sixteenth notes: four strokes per beat. Start very slowly, at 50 BPM, because sixteenth notes are twice as fast.

## Check yourself

- **Relaxed?** No tension in your forearms or shoulders.
- **Even?** Both hands sound the same.
- **On time?** Each note lands on the click.

If any of those fail, slow down by 10 BPM.
