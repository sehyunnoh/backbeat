Triplets have a rolling feel that flows well around the toms. If you play three notes per beat, one beat on each drum, you get a smooth cascade across the kit. Many classic drum fills use this idea.

You already know the triplet count from Level 6: **1 trip-let 2 trip-let 3 trip-let 4 trip-let**.

## The pattern

Bar 1 plays triplets on the snare to get the feel. Bar 2 gives each beat to a different drum: **Snare, Tom 1, Tom 2, Tom 3**. Alternate hands the whole time: R L R, L R L, R L R, L R L.

## Learn it in steps

1. **Triplets on one drum.** Play bar 1 until it is even. Say "1 trip-let 2 trip-let" out loud.
2. **Move after every three notes.** Do not rush the moves: each drum gets exactly one beat.
3. **Count the hands.** The first drum starts with the right hand (R L R), the second with the left (L R L) and so on, because the alternating never stops.
4. **Speed up very slowly.** Triplets need to stay even.

<Callout kind="note" title="Hand lead changes">
Because every beat has three notes, the hand that leads switches every beat. That is normal. If it feels awkward, practise it on one drum first, then add the moves.
</Callout>

## Use it in a fill

Play a bar of the basic 8-beat, then use this pattern as a fill in the next bar's last two beats (hands move from the hi-hat to Tom 2 and Tom 3), and land on the crash. Start slowly. The pulse should not change when you switch from eighth notes to triplets.

## Common problems

- **Triplets sound like eighth notes:** the notes must be exactly three per beat.
- **The moves are bumpy:** look at the next drum before you hit it.
- **You speed up:** keep the kick on every beat to anchor the pulse.
