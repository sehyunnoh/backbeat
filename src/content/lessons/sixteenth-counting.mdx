You already know quarter notes (**1 2 3 4**) and eighth notes (**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**). A **sixteenth note** is half of an eighth note: **four notes in every beat**.

## How to count them

Say **"1 e & a, 2 e & a, 3 e & a, 4 e & a"**. The number is the beat, the "&" is where the eighth note falls, and "e" and "a" fill the gaps.

| Note | Counted | Notes per beat |
|---|---|---|
| Quarter | 1 2 3 4 | 1 |
| Eighth | 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & | 2 |
| Sixteenth | 1 e & a 2 e & a ... | 4 |

Notice that sixteenth notes are not "fast" at a slow tempo. At 50 BPM the notes just come four at a time, and there is plenty of time between them.

## Play them

Press play and count with the chart. Then play it on one pad with alternating hands. Right hand on the numbers and the "&", left hand on the "e" and the "a".

This is the same single stroke roll you already know, just four notes in each beat instead of two.

## Add an accent on every beat

Playing a louder note on each number helps you feel the beat inside the stream of notes. Your right hand always lands on the number, so it carries the accent.

<Pattern
  title="Accent on every beat"

  minBpm={40}
  maxBpm={100}
  sn="OoooOoooOoooOooo"

<Callout kind="tip" title="Keep it slow">
If you cannot count it out loud, you are playing it too fast. At 50 BPM the sixteenth notes are as fast as eighth notes at 100 BPM, so go slowly and stay relaxed.
</Callout>

## Check yourself

- Do all four notes in each beat sound the same?
- Does your right-hand accent land exactly with the click?
- Can you keep counting the whole time?
