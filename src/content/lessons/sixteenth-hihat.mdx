In the basic 8-beat the hi-hat played eight notes per bar. Now it plays **sixteen**: every "1 e & a". The snare and kick stay in the same places, so only the hi-hat gets busier.

This sound is everywhere in pop, R&B and rock ballads, and it feels smooth and driving.

## Build it in layers again

### Layer 1: hi-hat only

Play the hi-hat with a relaxed wrist and count **1 e & a**. Hit the numbers a little harder than the rest. That gentle accent keeps the pulse clear.

### Layer 2: add the snare

Play the snare on 2 and 4 with your left hand, on top of the hi-hat. The hi-hat keeps going with your right hand, so for those two beats the left hand plays the snare while the right hand carries on.

## Which hand plays the hi-hat?

There are two common ways:

- **Right hand only.** Simple, but the right hand has to do all sixteen notes, which is tiring at first.
- **Alternating hands** (R L R L), with the left hand skipping to the snare when it needs to. It is more even and faster, but harder to coordinate.

Start with **right hand only** at 50 BPM. If it tires, slow down. Once it is steady, you can try alternating.

<Callout kind="note" title="Be patient">
This is one of the hardest steps so far. Your right hand is doing a lot. Short, regular practice helps more than one long, tense session.
</Callout>
